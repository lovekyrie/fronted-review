# High-frequency HR questions (frontend)

## 1. Self-intro (1–2 min)

Suggested structure:

1. Background (years + stack).
2. Flagship projects and core contributions (with numbers).
3. Why this role (business + tech).

## 2. High-frequency Qs

### 1) Strengths and weaknesses?

- Strength: a real example (e.g. you drove a perf program).
- Weakness: the fix you already run (e.g. how you manage communication cadence).

### 2) Why leave / why look?

- Career growth and harder problems. No dumping on the old team.

### 3) Conflict?

- Facts, options, outcome. No emotional play-by-play.

### 4) Why us?

- Business direction + technical challenge + your growth path.

## 3. Questions to ask them

- What is the team’s hardest technical problem right now?
- What is frontend’s role and decision power in the business?
- What does success look like in 3–6 months?

## 4. Principles

- Specific and real: examples, not slogans.
- Clear structure: context → action → result.
- Positive: no complaining about the last company or coworkers.

## 5. Templates you can reuse

### 1) Self-intro (60–90s)

```text
I have X years of frontend experience. Main stack: React/Vue plus the engineering toolchain.
The last two years I owned project A and project B. On A I led the performance work:
LCP from Xs to Ys, conversion up Z%.
I am looking for more complex product surfaces and more systematic engineering.
That matches XXX in your JD.
```

### 2) Why leave (safe version)

```text
The current team is fine. This is a stage choice, not a people problem.
Next I want to do performance and stability at a larger business scale,
and own the full path from design to production.
```

### 3) Strengths / weaknesses (don’t crash)

- Strength: something you can prove — perf, cross-team delivery, engineering standards.
- Weakness: something you already act on — e.g. “I used to over-invest in details; I now time-box with milestones and priority.”

## 6. HR pitfalls

1. Do not say “I learn fast” with no example or result.
2. Do not badmouth the last company or coworkers.
3. Be honest about money; do not make it the only reason.
4. On conflict: align on the goal first, then on the plan.
5. Gaps / job-hopping: one consistent story, no contradictions.
